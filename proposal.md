# Proposal: 

**Working title:** Analysis of global literacy, schooling, and women representation in government

**Summary of our dataset, questions, and audience**
Our website will compare data on literacy rates, average years of schooling, and women’s representation in government in order to determine patterns in education and female leadership. Our site’s audience will include people who are interested in education reform, students, educators, researchers, politicians, and the general public. 

**Short summary of data sets**
Our project includes three data sets from two sources. The first data set is on the share of women in local governments from around the world. It contains data on women specifically in national parliaments, and the data is expressed as a percentage of total positions available. We intend to use this data set from the year 2007 onwards in conjunction with the other two data sets in order to analyze how women in government are affected by literacy ratings and schooling
The next two data sets are from the same website on literacy rates graphed against average years of schooling. Each data set comes with needs for consideration: how is literacy measured? Is the intensity of schooling measured? In this case, literacy is measured as people who can read and write their own name, but not necessarily much more than that. The data about average years of schooling was measured with historical estimates up to the year 2015, beyond that they are projections from previous years. The data is altered by global trends derived from the UN. We intend to use data from both data sets from the year 2007 onwards and to contrast it against data from the data set of women in local governments.

**The main audience(s) of your website:**

Our audience includes people who are interested in education reform. This can include students, educators, researchers, and politicians. 
- Students may be interested in evaluating their own schooling and investigating possible unequal access to resources. They may be curious about how their education compares to that of their peers and want to learn more so that they can better advocate for themselves. 
- Educators may use our website to explore when education is most effective, consider where they may want to work, and potentially tailor their teaching to best help their students' learning.
- Researchers may be interested in topics such as education, poverty, gender equity, and economic development. They may use our website to gain insights into how literacy rates, years of schooling, and women leadership are connected to draw conclusions on larger trends in education and governance. 
- Politicians can use the website to inform themselves about education trends. They can use these insights to propose educational reforms or shape campaigns related to education policy.
